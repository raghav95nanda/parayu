# Native form | contextual gloss | teaching note | optional text=meaning / text=meaning
സുഖം|comfort; health; well-being|Borrowed from Sanskrit sukha (“ease / happiness”). In a reply to “sukhamaano?”, sukham naturally means “I’m well”. The final -m is part of the word here, not a question ending.
സുഖമാണോ|Are you well?|Sukham + aanu becomes sukhamaanu (“am / are well”); aanu + the yes/no ending -o becomes aano. The person is understood from context. Literally “Well-being — is it?”; naturally “How are you?”|sukham=comfort; health; well-being (from Sanskrit sukha, ease / happiness) / aano?=is / are …? (aanu + question ending -o)
ആണ്|is; am; are|Aanu is the linking verb for identity or description. The same form works with I, you, and they; it is not a word for “you”.
ആണോ|is / are …?|Aanu (“is / are”) becomes aano when the question ending -o is added.|aanu=is / am / are / -o?=yes/no question ending
ഉണ്ടോ|is there …?; do you have …?|Undu (“there is / exists”) + -o makes undo? In a possession context, natural English uses “have”.|undu=there is; exists / -o?=yes/no question ending
വേണം|want; need|The person is often left out when obvious. Enikku … venam is literally “to me … is wanted / needed”, naturally “I want / need …”.
വേണ്ട|do not want; do not need|A negative form learned as a unit. Do not split it into venam + illa: the negative has its own form.
നമുക്ക്|to / for us (including you)|This is the “to / for” form of nammal. With pokaam it makes an invitation: “let us go”. The pronoun changes shape rather than keeping nammal unchanged.
എനിക്ക്|to me; for me|This is the “to / for” form of njaan (“I”). Malayalam uses it for wants, needs, abilities and experiences. It is not simply njaan with an ending stuck on unchanged.
എന്നെ|me (object)|The object form of njaan (“I”): use it for the person an action affects, as in “help me”.
എന്റെ|my|The possessive form of njaan (“I”). Put ente before the thing or person: ente peru = my name.
നിന്റെ|your (informal)|The possessive form of nee (“you”, informal). Use ningalude for polite or plural “your”.
നിങ്ങളുടെ|your (polite / plural)|The possessor comes first: ningalude phone = your phone.|ningal=you (polite / plural) / -ude=of; possessive ending
ഞങ്ങളുടെ|our (excluding you)|The possessive form of njangal: it refers to our group without including the person being addressed.|njangal=we (excluding the listener) / -ude=of; possessive ending
നമ്മുടെ|our (including you)|The possessive form of nammal (“we”, including the listener). Learn the changed pronoun form as a unit.
അവരുടെ|their; his / her (respectful)|Avar can mean “they” or refer respectfully to one person.|avar=they; he / she respectfully / -ude=of; possessive ending
ഞങ്ങൾക്ക്|to / for us (excluding you)|The group excludes the person being addressed.|njangal=we (excluding the listener) / -kku=to / for
ഇതിന്റെ|of this; its|Ithu changes to ithin- before this possessive ending. Learn ithinte as the possessive of ithu.
അതിന്റെ|of that; its|Athu changes to athin- before this possessive ending. Learn athinte as the possessive of athu.
ഇതിന്|for this; to this|The “to / for” form of ithu (“this”). Pronouns can change their stem in case forms.
ഇതിൽ|in this|The final short vowel changes when the ending joins.|ithu=this / -il=in
ഇതിലും|than this; in this too|In size comparisons this is naturally “than this”; in other contexts -um can mean “also”. The gloss depends on the comparison.|ithil=in this / -um=also; in this comparison, helps give “than this”
എനിക്കും|to me too|The -um adds “also / too” to enikku.|enikku=to me / -um=also; too
കുട്ടിക്കും|to / for the child too|The dative -kku and additive -um can stack.|kutti=child / -kku=to / for / -um=also; too
എവിടെയാണ്|where is …?|Evide asks where; aanu links the thing and its location. A y joins the vowels. The question is supplied by evide, so there is no -o here.|evide=where / aanu=is / are (with a linking y)
എവിടേക്ക്|to where?|A direction ending turns a location question into a destination question.|evide=where / -ekku=towards; to
എന്താണ്|what is …?|Enthu (“what”) supplies the question; aanu is still the ordinary linking verb.|enthu=what / aanu=is / are
എത്രയാണ്|how much is …?|Ethra asks how much; aanu links the amount to the item. The y is a joining sound.|ethra=how much; how many / aanu=is / are
ഏതാണ്|which is …?|Ethu is the question word. There is no need for the yes/no ending -o.|ethu=which / aanu=is / are
ആരാണ്|who is …?|Aaru asks who. Aanu links the identity being asked about.|aaru=who / aanu=is / are
എന്നാണ്|is called; is that …|Ennu introduces a name or quoted content. In ente peru … ennaanu, the whole expression names someone.|ennu=that; called (quotation marker) / aanu=is / are
നിന്നാണ്|is from …|Ninnu follows a place marked with -il. Aanu can attach to focus the source: “it is from …”.|ninnu=from / aanu=is / are
മണിക്കാണ്|is at … o’clock|Manikku marks the clock time; aanu links or focuses it.|mani=hour; clock time / -kku=at / aanu=is / are
വീട്ടിലാണ്|is at home / in the house|Veedu changes to veett- before -il. This is a real stem change, not a new word.|veedu=house; home (stem veett-) / -il=in; at / aanu=is / are
മാസത്തിലാണ്|is in the month|Maasam changes to maasath- before -il.|maasam=month (stem maasath-) / -il=in / aanu=is / are
പ്ലാറ്റ്ഫോമിലാണ്|is on the platform|The place name takes -il before aanu.|platform=platform / -il=on / aanu=is / are
ആദ്യമായാണ്|is for the first time|Aadyamaayi is the “for the first time” expression; aanu can attach for focus.|aadyamaayi=for the first time / aanu=is / are
കണ്ടതിൽ|in having met / seen|The past “saw / met” is made into an event expression. Kandathil santhosham is the conventional “pleasure in meeting [you]”.|kandathu=having seen / met; what was seen / -il=in
തരാമോ|could you give me …?|Tharaam is a willing / possible giving form. Adding -o makes a polite request; -aam does not always mean ability alone.|thar-=give (towards me / us) / -aam=can / willing-to form in this request / -o?=yes/no question ending
തരുമോ|will you give …?|The future / non-past form tharum becomes a request when questioned.|thar-=give (towards me / us) / -um=will / non-past form / -o?=yes/no question ending
കഴിയും|can; is possible|With a verb in -aan and a person in the “to / for” form, kazhiyum expresses ability: enikku … kazhiyum = I can … . Learn this modal form as a unit.
അറിയാം|know|Often used with a “to / for” pronoun: enikku ariyaam = I know. Here -aam is part of this knowing form; do not translate every -aam as “let us”.
അറിയില്ല|do not know|This is the negative of knowing. Its spoken shape is learned as ariyilla.|ariy-=know / -illa=negative: not
അറിയാമോ|do you know …?|The person is supplied by context.|ariyaam=know / -o?=yes/no question ending
വരാം|can come; will come (offer)|The -aam form can express willingness, possibility or an offer. Context decides.|var-=come / -aam=can / willing-to form
കാണാം|can see; see you|In leave-taking, naale kaanaam conventionally means “see you tomorrow”, rather than a literal statement of visual ability.|kaan-=see / -aam=can / let us / willing-to form, according to context
പോകാം|can go; let us go|With namukku, this makes “let us go”; without it, context can make it “I can go”.|pok-=go / -aam=can / let us / willing-to form
കഴിക്കാം|can eat; let us eat|Context determines whether this is an ability, suggestion or invitation.|kazhikk-=eat / -aam=can / let us / willing-to form
ഉപയോഗിക്കാം|can use|The verb comes after the thing you want to use.|upayogikk-=use / -aam=can / willing-to form
തുറക്കാം|can open; let us open||thurakk-=open / -aam=can / let us form
കാത്തിരിക്കാം|can wait||kaathirikk-=wait / -aam=can / willing-to form
ചികിത്സിക്കാം|can treat||chikithsikk-=treat medically / -aam=can / willing-to form
കണ്ടു|saw; met|This is a changed past stem of kaanuka (“to see”). Learn kaanuka → kandu; do not build it by adding an English-style past ending.
വന്നു|came; has arrived|An irregular past form of varuka (“come”). Learn varuka → vannu as a pair.
പറഞ്ഞു|said; told|A past form of parayuka (“say”). The stem changes: paray- → paranj-.
അടച്ചു|closed|A past form of adaykkuka (“close”). The ending and stem change together.
തീർന്നു|finished; ended|A past form of theeruka (“finish / end”). Learn the changed past form as a unit.
എത്തി|arrived; reached|A past form of eththuka (“reach / arrive”).
കിട്ടി|got; received|A past form of kittuka (“get / receive”).
കൊടുത്തു|gave|A past form of kodukkuka (“give”). The doubled kk changes in the past form.
വർദ്ധിച്ചു|increased|A past form of vardhikkuka (“increase”).
ഉണ്ടായി|occurred; came into being|A past form of undaakuka (“happen / come into being”).
വന്നാൽ|if … come / comes|The conditional uses the past stem vann- with -aal; it need not refer to past time.|vann-=come (changed stem from varuka) / -aal=if; conditional ending
ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ|if there is …|The condition is built on undu (“there is”).|undu=there is / -enkil=if
ഉണ്ടാകും|will be; there will be|A future form of undaakuka (“be / happen”). Do not confuse this with a fixed possession statement.
പോകേണ്ട|do not go; need not go|A negative necessity / instruction form. Learn the whole form; it is not the future pokum with -illa added.
കൊടുക്കണോ|should I give …?|The necessity form kodukkanam is questioned as kodukkano: a useful way to ask what to do.|kodukk-=give / -ano?=should / need to …? (questioned necessity form)
ഊരണോ|should I remove …?|The necessity form ooranam becomes oorano in a question.|oor-=remove / -ano?=should / need to …? (questioned necessity form)
മറക്കരുത്|do not forget|The negative command ending is not the same as -illa.|marakk-=forget / -aruthu=do not (prohibition)
കേടാക്കരുത്|do not damage||kedaakk-=damage / -aruthu=do not (prohibition)
സമയമില്ല|there is no time|Illa negates existence / availability.|samayam=time / illa=there is no; not available
സുഖമില്ല|not well|This is the absence of well-being; the natural English translation is “I do not feel well”.|sukham=well-being / illa=not; absence
മനസ്സിലായി|understood|Manassilaakuka (“understand”) literally involves something becoming clear “in the mind”. Learn the changed past form manassilaayi.|manassil=in the mind (manassu + -il) / aayi=became; came to be
മനസ്സിലായില്ല|did not understand|The positive past is manassilaayi. The negative uses a changed joined form with -illa.|manassil=in the mind / aay-=became clear; understood (past stem) / -illa=not; negative
കേട്ടില്ല|did not hear|The positive past is kettu (“heard”); the negative uses kett- + illa.|kett-=heard (past stem of kelkkuka) / -illa=not; negative
കാണുന്നില്ല|not seeing; cannot find|In this context, “my bag is not being seen” naturally means “I cannot find my bag”.|kaan-=see / -unnilla=negative present: not seeing
കിട്ടുന്നില്ല|not getting; not available||kitt-=get; receive / -unnilla=negative present: not getting
പോയിട്ടില്ല|have not gone|The perfect negative says the experience has not occurred; poyi is the changed past of pokuka.|poy-=went (changed stem of pokuka) / -ittilla=have not; perfect negative
തുറന്നിട്ടുണ്ടോ|is it open?; has it been opened?|This asks whether the result of opening is in effect. Learn the full opened-state expression before trying other verbs.|thurann-=opened (changed stem of thurakkuka) / -ittundu=has been; resulting state / -o?=yes/no question ending
തുറന്നിരിക്കും|will be open|The opened state is combined with an auxiliary form of “be”.|thurannu=opened / irikkum=will be; will remain
അടഞ്ഞിരിക്കുന്നു|is closed|An intransitive closed state, not the action of closing something.|adanju=became closed / irikkunnu=is; remains (state auxiliary)
വരുന്നുണ്ട്|is coming|The present form joins undu to emphasize the ongoing occurrence.|varunnu=is coming / undu=exists; emphasizes the occurrence here
പഠിക്കുകയാണ്|am learning / studying|This focuses an action in progress. The citation / verbal-noun form joins aanu with a linking y.|padikkuka=learning / studying (verbal noun) / aanu=is / am / are; ongoing-action construction
താമസിക്കുന്നത്|living / staying; where one lives|The present action is turned into a noun-like expression. In “where is it that you live?”, it identifies the act of living.|thaamasikk-=live; stay / -unnathu=the act / fact of …ing (present nominalized form)
പോകുന്നതിന്|for going; to go|A nominalized present action takes a “to / for” ending.|pok-=go / -unnathinu=for the act of going (nominalized form + dative)
പറഞ്ഞത്|what was said|The past action is made into a thing / fact that can be discussed.|paranj-=said (past stem of parayuka) / -athu=what was …; nominalizing ending
വരച്ചത്|what was drawn; drew|A past action is nominalized: “who is it that drew [this]?”|varachch-=drew (past stem of varaykkuka) / -athu=what was …; nominalizing ending
പൂർത്തിയായി|became complete; is completed|Aayi indicates a change of state, rather than a simple identity with aanu.|poorthi=completion / aayi=became; came to be
ഇഷ്ടമായി|liked; came to like|With enikku (“to me”), this naturally becomes “I liked it”.|ishtam=liking / aayi=became; came to be
പച്ചയായി|green; as green|Here aayi turns the quality into a state / manner expression.|pachcha=green / aayi=as; in the state of
പൊതിഞ്ഞു|wrapped; packed|A past / linking form of pothiyuka (“wrap”). Before tharaamo it means “could you wrap [it] and give [it to me]?”
ഇട്ടു|put; wore|A past / linking form of iduka (“put / wear”). Ittu nokkaamo = may I put it on and see / try it?
മാറ്റി|changed; exchanged|A linking form of maattuka (“change”). Maatti tharaamo asks someone to exchange an item and give it back.
തെറ്റി|went wrong; strayed|Vazhi thetti is the expression for losing your way: “the route went wrong”.
നഷ്ടപ്പെട്ടു|was lost|A lexical expression for something getting lost; not an instruction to lose it.
ചോദിക്കട്ടെ|let me ask|The -atte form asks permission / expresses a wish to do something.|chodikk-=ask / -atte=let me; permission / wish ending
നോക്കട്ടെ|let me look||nokk-=look / -atte=let me; permission / wish ending
ആദ്യമായി|for the first time|Aadyam (“first”) is used in an adverbial expression with aayi.
ഇന്നത്തെ|today’s|The form qualifies a following noun as belonging to today.|innu=today / -athe=of; belonging to (adjectival time form)
സംഘത്തിലെ|of / in the group|A group’s location / membership relation is made into a modifier.|samgham=group / -thile=in; of (locative modifier)
കോഴ്സിന്റെ|of the course||course=course / -inte=of; possessive ending
ചെരുപ്പുകളാണ്|are shoes / footwear||cheruppu=shoe; footwear / -kal=plural / aanu=is / are
പുസ്തകങ്ങളാണ്|are books||pusthakam=book (stem pusthaka-) / -ngal=plural / aanu=is / are
സുരക്ഷിതമാണോ|is it safe?||surakshitham=safe / aano?=is / are …? (aanu + -o)
ശരിയാണോ|is it correct?||shari=correct; right / aano?=is / are …? (aanu + -o)
ലഭ്യമാണോ|is it available?||labhyam=available / aano?=is / are …? (aanu + -o)
സസ്യാഹാരം|vegetarian food|A useful compound: sasya refers to plants and aahaaram to food. The vowels join in sasyaahaaram.|sasya=plant; plant-based / aahaaram=food
തലവേദന|headache||thala=head / vedana=pain
ചൂടുവെള്ളം|hot water||choodu=heat; hot / vellam=water
നമസ്കാരം|hello; respectful greeting|A conventional greeting with Sanskrit origins. Learn the greeting as one unit rather than translating its syllables separately.
നന്ദി|thanks; gratitude|A complete conventional thank-you. There is no need to add a separate word for “you”.

മുന്നോട്ട്|forward; ahead||mun=front / -ottu=towards
ഇത്രയും|this much; so much|An extent expression; the ending belongs to this established form.
പരസ്പരം|each other; mutually|Shows that the action goes both ways, as in helping one another.
ആകെ|altogether; total|Qualifies an amount as a total.
പേർ|people (when counting)|Use per for counting people, rather than translating “heads” literally.
അനു|Anu (a person’s name)|A proper name; keep it as one unit.
രാഘവ്|Raghav (a person’s name)|Replace this name with yours; personal names do not need a grammar split.
